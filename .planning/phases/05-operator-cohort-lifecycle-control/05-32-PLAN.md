## The defect, stated precisely

Round 3 gave `pollDetail` a round guard and left `refreshCohorts` without one. Its ok branch, at
`packages/web/src/stores/operator.ts:907-925`, writes `cohorts`, `rows`, `metrics`, `health`,
`operatorActions`, `defaults`, `listStale` and `lastUpdated` with no re-check that the session that
asked is still the session that is running. Its unreachable branch, at line 901, writes `listStale`
the same way.

The window is ordinary, not exotic. `OperatorConsole.tsx:13` polls the list every 4000 ms and
`packages/web/src/lib/operator.ts:12` gives each read an 8000 ms timeout, so two reads are routinely
in flight at once. If the fast one 401s and the slow one succeeds, `expireSession` runs first and the
late ok write puts the whole gated slice back. `signOut` has the identical exposure: a poll issued
just before the click lands just after the clear.

The reviewer reproduced it against the shipped store, observing `auth: logged-out` alongside a
repopulated cohort list, a served health object and a `listStale: false` stamp.

`pollDetail` is only incidentally immune: `expireSession` sets `view` back to the list, and its
cohort-keyed guard rejects on that. That is precisely the asymmetry worth closing, because the
immunity is a side effect of another field rather than a rule anybody stated.

What it costs is not cosmetic. The store's `signOut` comment says the next session must re-read the
mode "rather than render a stale mode claim against a service that may have been restarted into
another mode", `expireSession` is documented as clearing every gated slice, and the `SESSION_EXPIRED`
copy promises the operator that "Monitoring rebuilds from this service's state after you sign in".
After a late write, the next sign-in renders the previous session's cohort list, metrics and
broadcast-mode chip until the first read of the new session lands, and the broadcast-mode chip in
particular is a claim about whether this service can move money.

The store already knows the pattern is needed: `advertise` checks `get().auth === 'logged-in'` before
opening a drill-down.

## Why the 401 branch stays first

The obvious reading is that a guard belongs at the top, so the opposite choice needs stating.

A guard may only precede a branch whose subject is narrower than its own. In `pollDetail` the guard
is COHORT-scoped and the 401 branch is SESSION-scoped, which is wider, so the branch runs first and
05-28 pinned that with its own row. Here the guard is session-scoped and the 401 branch is
session-scoped too, so nothing is lost by either order for the case that matters: an answer that
arrives while the session is live is guarded by neither, and an answer that arrives afterwards has
nothing left to expire.

Keeping the branch where it is therefore costs nothing and keeps both read paths reading the same
way, which is worth more than a micro-optimization. One consequence is left deliberately unchanged
and is recorded rather than fixed: a 401 landing after an explicit sign-out still replaces the clean
signed-out state with the session-expired banner. That is pre-existing, it is not what W5 describes
(a 401 repopulates nothing), and it is not in this round's owner-approved scope.
