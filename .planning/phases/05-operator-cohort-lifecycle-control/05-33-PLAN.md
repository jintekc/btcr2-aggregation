## The defect, stated precisely

### CR-02, the settings-save byte wedge

Three limits govern one field, and they disagree by roughly five times.

The `TERMS_TEXT` boot seed is bounded at 20000 characters by `textKnob`
(`runtime-settings.ts:615-623`, added by 05-31). `applySettings` refuses above the same 20000
characters (`runtime-settings.ts:775-777`). And the gated `PUT /v1/operator/settings` route is
wrapped in `bodyLimit({ maxSize: 4 * 1024, ... })` at `hono-adapter.ts:904`, which runs DURING
streaming, before `c.req.json()` ever buffers the body.

The console posts the WHOLE form on every save, by design: `settingsPatch`
(`SettingsView.tsx:93-103`) unconditionally includes `termsText: form.termsText`, seeded from the
served snapshot by `formFromSnapshot` at line 77. So the body carries the stored terms whether or
not the operator touched that field.

Reproduced during planning, byte-exact against the real patch shape, independently of the review's
own numbers and matching them:

| terms characters | request body bytes | route answer |
|---|---|---|
| (none) | 184 | accepted |
| 3900 | 4084 | accepted |
| 4000 | 4184 | 413 |
| 20000 (the stored ceiling) | 20184 | 413 |

The operator consequence is exactly the harm round 4 closed one door of, at a value round 4
explicitly made storable. Set `TERMS_TEXT` at boot to a genuine 5000 character participation
document, roughly 850 words, comfortably inside the documented cap. The holder stores it. The join
flow works with it. Then open Settings to rename the service, and the save is refused with
`request too large`, a message naming no field, no limit and no remedy. Every later save fails the
same way, whichever field is edited, until the process restarts with shorter terms. That is the
literal text of roadmap SC3 and of the "without editing env vars or restarting" clause of SVC-04.

It is not latent. `packages/service/tests/lifecycle-routes.spec.ts:577-582` already pins the 413 as
correct behavior for an 8 KiB terms body, so the wedge is asserted and shipped. And
`docs/DEPLOY.md:351-355` and `:509`, both added by 05-31, now state a 20000 character cap "on both
paths that set them" with a runtime save above it "refused with the limit named". Both sentences are
false of the console save path.

### Why the review's proposed multiplier is not enough, measured

The review proposes `SETTINGS_BODY_LIMIT_BYTES = MAX_TERMS_CHARS * 2 + 4096`, which is 44096 bytes.
That fixes ASCII and it fixes surrogate pairs. Measured during planning, it does NOT fix a terms
document written in a script that costs three UTF-8 bytes per character:

| terms document at the 20000 character ceiling | body bytes | answer under a 44096 budget |
|---|---|---|
| ASCII | 20184 | accepted |
| surrogate pairs (emoji) | 40184 | accepted |
| three-byte-per-character script | 60184 | 413 |

Shipping the `* 2` parenthetical would therefore leave this gap OPEN for any operator writing their
own participation terms in their own language, and would leave it open in exactly the form this
round exists to end: a number chosen rather than derived from what it has to carry. The plan
derives the multiplier instead, and states the derivation in the source. This is a deliberate,
visible divergence from the review's suggested constant, not from its direction: the direction is
the verifier's stated `missing` item, "the route's bodyLimit computed from `MAX_TERMS_CHARS` plus
headroom for the rest of the form and JSON escaping", and the derivation is how that is satisfied
honestly.

### Round-4 prohibition explicitly SUPERSEDED

`05-31-PLAN.md` prohibited exporting `MAX_SERVICE_NAME_CHARS` or `MAX_TERMS_CHARS`. That was a
round-4 SCOPE FENCE (keep the module surface still while bounding two seeds), not standing law, and
the owner-approved CR-02 direction requires the route to consume a budget derived from the terms
cap. This plan therefore exports `SETTINGS_BODY_LIMIT_BYTES` and `MAX_TERMS_CHARS`, and only those
two. `MAX_SERVICE_NAME_CHARS` stays unexported, because nothing outside the module needs it and the
round-4 reasoning still holds for it. Stated here so neither the executor nor the next verifier
reads the export as a violation of a live prohibition.

## The rule this plan holds

**A limit that governs a field belongs to that field, derived once, at the place the field is
defined. Any other layer that has to bound the same thing derives its number from that one, and a
number nobody derived may never be the thing that answers.**
