## What the defect actually is, stated precisely

`pollDetail` reads `view.cohortId` once, before the await, and writes the answer into the shared
`detail` slot afterwards with no re-check. `openCohort` re-points the view and clears `detail`, but
neither it nor `CohortDetail`'s effect cancels or invalidates a request already in flight for the
previous cohort. The window is that request's remaining latency, bounded by the client's 8000 ms
timeout.

The harm is not a cosmetic mis-render, because the drill-down splits its two inputs.
`LifecycleActions` receives `cohortId` as a PROP (cohort B, the one on screen) and reads `detail`
from the STORE (cohort A, the late answer). `cancelAvailability`, `cancelRung`,
`finalizeAvailability`, `detail.seatsJoined` and `detail.funding` are all derived from A, while
`onConfirm` fires `cancelCohort(baseUrl, cohortId)` against B. If A is unfunded and B is funded, the
operator is shown the rung-3 panel (no typed confirmation, no recovery-key disclosure, A's seat
count) and one click cancels the funded cohort B. That is exactly the outcome the D-03 ceremony
ladder exists to prevent, and its cost is stranded Bitcoin at B's beacon address under a throwaway
recovery key.

Both barriers in this plan are needed and neither is redundant. The round guard stops the wrong data
from being painted. The component refusal stops the ceremony from rendering at all if some future
path ever paints it anyway, which is the difference between a bug that strands funds and a bug that
renders an empty card.
